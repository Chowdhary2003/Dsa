# Revision guide

The goal of revision is to retrieve the solution from memory, not to reread it passively.

## A single revision session

For each problem:

1. Hide the solution and restate the problem in your own words.
2. Identify the likely pattern or core idea before writing code.
3. Write the solution from memory. Do not peek after every line.
4. Test a normal case and at least one boundary or unusual case.
5. State the time and auxiliary-space complexity and explain why they apply.
6. Reopen the note, compare, and write down the one thing you missed.
7. Update the [revision tracker](REVISION-TRACKER.md).

## Suggested spaced-revision checkpoints

| Checkpoint | What to do |
|---|---|
| First pass (Day 0) | Solve, understand, and record the learning date. |
| Day 1 | Rebuild the solution without looking. |
| Day 7 | Solve again and explain the approach and complexity aloud. |
| Day 30 | Do a final recall check; keep it in the regular rotation if still difficult. |

These are starting intervals, not strict rules. Bring a problem forward when you cannot recall the approach, make repeated mistakes, or need hints. Extend the interval when you can solve and explain it reliably.

## Confidence scale

| Score | Meaning | Next action |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Cannot recall the approach | Relearn, then retry soon |
| 2 | Recognize the solution but need substantial hints | Practice again within 1–2 days |
| 3 | Can solve with a small hint or after a slow start | Revisit within a week |
| 4 | Can solve and explain without help | Keep the scheduled checkpoint |
| 5 | Can solve, explain, and handle variations independently | Move to occasional maintenance |

## Keep revision efficient

- Prioritize low-confidence and overdue problems instead of reviewing every note equally.
- Attempt first; open the code only after a genuine attempt.
- Record a short, specific mistake (for example, “forgot the empty-input case”), not a long diary entry.
- If a solution has multiple approaches, compare their trade-offs rather than memorizing only one implementation.
- Mark a problem as learned only after solving it without looking at the note.
